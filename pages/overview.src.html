Damu Grid is a working prototype that helps blood banks share platelet stock across a county, even when facilities report days late. The engine suggests; a person decides.

The problem

Units expire on one shelf while a patient waits at another.

Kenya collects far fewer units of blood than it needs. The 2026 Strathmore study in The Lancet Global Health looked at Siaya, Nakuru and Turkana and found that blood is often stored far from where patients need it.

Platelets make this sharpest: they last five days. Collection happens mostly at regional centres, while requests arrive at county hospitals. Rural facilities often report late, so the coordinator decides on counts that are days old.

The solution · three steps, every day
  1. 1
    Know today's stock.For each facility, take the last report and roll it forward to today using expected usage. Usage is assumed on the high side, so the hub never counts on units that may already be gone.
  2. 2
    Suggest the moves that matter.Units that would expire before local patients use them go where they will be used in time. Facilities below two days of safety stock are topped up, oldest units first. Every suggestion says why in one sentence.
  3. 3
    Confirm before anything moves.The sending facility checks each request against its own shelf. It always releases units that would expire anyway and never gives away its own safety stock, so a unit can never be promised twice.
People stay in control. A blood bank officer approves or rejects every suggestion. Rejections are recorded.
The proof

Tested on 20 simulated years it had never seen.

Cost per unit of demand combines expired units, unmet requests and transfers. Lower is better. A lower bound, computed as if the whole future were known, shows the remaining headroom.

  • With fresh reports, coordination cuts cost by about 85% compared with no coordination (2.024 → 0.304).
  • With 2–4 day reporting delays, estimating today's stock roughly halves cost again.
  • When supply is scarce and sits mostly in Nairobi, a full optimiser beats the simple rule (1.230 vs 1.635), so the hub runs both. With fresh reports the two tie.
  • Units promised twice drop to zero once senders confirm each move.
  • With ABO/RhD blood groups (Kenyan donor mix), coordination still cuts expired units by 56% and unmet requests by 70%. Each patient gets the oldest compatible unit, and RhD-negative units are kept for RhD-negative patients unless they would expire.
  • The browser demo and the Python engine give identical results, checked on 45 configurations; 50 automated tests.
